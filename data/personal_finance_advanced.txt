ADVANCED PERSONAL FINANCE — INDIA-SPECIFIC GUIDE

=== MUTUAL FUND CATEGORIES & SELECTION ===

EQUITY FUNDS:
Large Cap: Top 100 companies by market cap. Stable, lower risk. Returns ~11-13% CAGR. HDFC Top 100, Mirae Asset Large Cap.
Mid Cap: 101-250 companies. Higher growth potential. ~13-16% CAGR. Returns volatile. Nippon Mid Cap, Kotak Emerging Equity.
Small Cap: 251+ companies. Highest risk & reward. ~14-18% CAGR. Only for 7+ year horizon. SBI Small Cap, Quant Small Cap.
Flexi Cap: Manager can invest across market caps. Good for beginners. Parag Parikh Flexi Cap, Canara Robeco Flexi Cap.
ELSS: Tax-saving equity fund. 3-year lock-in. 80C eligible. Mirae Asset ELSS, Quant ELSS.
Index Funds: Track Nifty 50 / Sensex. Lowest expense ratio (0.1-0.2%). Nifty 50 index funds: UTI, Nippon, HDFC.
Sector Funds: IT, Banking, Pharma, Auto specific. High risk — only if you understand sector. Not for beginners.

DEBT FUNDS:
Liquid Fund: Money market instruments. 7-8% return. Best for emergency fund + short-term parking.
Ultra Short Duration: 1-6 month instruments. 7-8.5% return.
Short Duration Fund: 1-3 year instruments. 7-9% return. Good for 1-2 year money.
Gilt Fund: Only government securities. No credit risk. Returns vary with interest rate changes.

HYBRID FUNDS:
Balanced Advantage Fund (BAF): Dynamic equity-debt allocation. ~10-12% CAGR. Less volatile than pure equity. Good for moderate risk investors. HDFC BAF, ICICI Prudential BAF.
Aggressive Hybrid: 65-80% equity, 20-35% debt. Suitable for SIP investment.

DIRECT vs REGULAR PLANS:
Direct plans have NO distributor commission. Expense ratio 0.5-1% lower than regular.
Over 20 years, 1% lower expense ratio can mean 25-40% higher corpus. Always choose DIRECT plans.
Platforms: MF Utilities, AMC websites, Coin by Zerodha, Groww, Kuvera (all charge zero for direct MF)

SIP STRATEGIES:
Step-up SIP: Increase SIP by 10-15% each year (matching salary increment). Dramatically improves corpus.
If ₹5,000 SIP with 10% annual step-up for 20 years (12% return) = ~₹1.5Cr vs ₹50L without step-up.
SIP date: Choose 5th-7th of month (after salary credit, before markets get directional bias)
SIP in market crash: Best time! Lower NAV means more units. Never stop SIP in a crash.

LUMP SUM INVESTMENT:
In volatile market: Use Systematic Transfer Plan (STP) — park in liquid fund, then transfer monthly to equity MF.
Reduces timing risk. Transfer over 6-12 months.

=== STOCK MARKET BASICS ===

INDIAN STOCK EXCHANGES:
NSE (National Stock Exchange): Nifty 50 index. Larger equity volumes. NSCCL clears trades.
BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange): Sensex (30 stocks). Oldest exchange (1875). Both settle in T+1.

INDICES:
Nifty 50: Top 50 companies by free-float market cap. India's benchmark.
Sensex: Top 30 BSE companies. India's oldest index.
Nifty Next 50: Companies ranked 51-100. Good for passive investing via index funds.
Nifty Midcap 150, Nifty Smallcap 250: Broader market indices.

HOW TO START INVESTING IN STOCKS:
1. Open Demat + Trading account: Zerodha (₹200 flat brokerage), Groww, Upstox, Angel One
2. Complete KYC with PAN, Aadhaar, bank account
3. Link savings account for funds transfer
4. Start with large-cap stocks or Nifty 50 index ETF (NIFTYBEES)
5. Never invest money you may need in 1-2 years in equity

FUNDAMENTAL ANALYSIS BASICS:
P/E Ratio (Price/Earnings): Lower is cheaper relative to earnings. Compare within same sector.
P/B Ratio (Price/Book): Below 1 may indicate undervalued. Book value = company's net assets.
Dividend Yield: Annual dividend / Share price × 100. Income from holding shares.
Return on Equity (ROE): Net profit / Shareholders equity. Higher = better management.
Debt-to-Equity: Lower is safer. Above 2-3 = highly leveraged, risky.
EPS (Earnings per Share): Higher and growing EPS = healthy company.

NIFTY 50 INVESTING — SIMPLEST STRATEGY:
Invest monthly in Nifty 50 index fund via SIP. Don't try to pick stocks initially.
Historical Nifty 50 return: ~12-13% CAGR over any 15+ year period.
This strategy beats 90% of active fund managers over long periods.

=== BEHAVIOURAL FINANCE & MONEY PSYCHOLOGY ===

COMMON MONEY MISTAKES INDIANS MAKE:
- Keeping all money in savings account (3.5-4% vs 12% equity return over time)
- Breaking FD/PF in emergencies (emergency fund should prevent this)
- Buying endowment/money-back policies for "safe" insurance+investment (worst of both worlds)
- Waiting for "right time" to invest (time in market > timing the market)
- Following tips on WhatsApp/YouTube for stock picks (recipe for losses)
- Not having term insurance at all (₹1Cr term cover at 30 costs ₹8-12K/year only)
- Investing in real estate with all savings (illiquid, low rental yield of 2-3%)

RULE OF 72: Divide 72 by annual return % = years to double money.
7% return → doubles in 10.3 years
12% return → doubles in 6 years
24% CC interest → debt doubles in 3 years (devastating!)

INFLATION REALITY CHECK:
₹1,00,000 today at 6% inflation = ₹57,000 in 10 years (real value halves in ~12 years)
A savings account at 4% return when inflation is 6% = NEGATIVE real return of -2%
Equity is the only mainstream asset class that reliably beats inflation long-term in India

DOLLAR-COST AVERAGING (SIP principle):
Invest fixed amount every month regardless of market level.
In market highs: fewer units bought. In market lows: more units bought.
Average cost per unit reduces over time. Eliminates need to time the market.

=== INSURANCE PLANNING ===

TERM INSURANCE (Priority #1 for breadwinners):
How much: 10-15x annual income MINIMUM. If income is ₹10L/year → ₹1-1.5Cr cover.
Type: Pure term plan only. Avoid ULIP (mixed product, bad at both insurance and investment).
Age matters: ₹1Cr cover costs ~₹8,000-12,000/year at age 30; ~₹20,000-30,000/year at age 40.
Claim settlement ratio: Check IRDAI data. LIC (98.7%), Max Life (99.5%), HDFC Life (99.4%).
Riders: Accidental death benefit, critical illness rider optional but useful.
Term: 30-35 years or till age 60, whichever applies.

HEALTH INSURANCE (Priority #2):
Individual: Minimum ₹5L; family floater: ₹10-25L.
Cashless hospitals: Check network hospitals near you before buying.
Waiting period: Pre-existing diseases covered after 2-4 years (varies by policy).
Best insurers: Niva Bupa (formerly Max Bupa), Star Health, HDFC Ergo, Care Health.
Avoid: Policies with co-payment clauses, sub-limits on room rent.
Parents: Buy separate policy for parents (senior citizen plans). Parents can drain family floater quickly.

THINGS NOT TO BUY:
Endowment/money-back plans: ~4-6% return after all charges. Terrible. Buy term + invest in MF instead.
ULIP: High charges (5-7% first few years), complex, inferior returns.
Accidental insurance only: Doesn't cover illness. Health insurance is more important.

=== FINANCIAL PLANNING BY LIFE STAGE ===

AGE 20-25 (Early Career):
- Emergency fund: 3 months expenses (liquid fund)
- Term insurance: Buy now (cheapest premiums)
- Health insurance: Don't rely on employer — get personal cover
- SIP: Even ₹500/month in index fund is a great start
- Avoid debt except education loan (legitimate investment in yourself)
- Focus: Income growth, skill building, avoid lifestyle inflation

AGE 26-35 (Growth Phase):
- Emergency fund: 6 months expenses
- SIP: 20%+ of income in equity MFs
- PPF: Max ₹1.5L/year for tax-free debt component
- NPS: Start for extra ₹50K deduction (old regime) + retirement corpus
- Home loan: EMI must be ≤40% of income; 20% down payment minimum
- Child planning: SSY for girl child, ELSS for education corpus

AGE 36-45 (Peak Earning):
- Aggressive wealth accumulation phase
- Step-up SIP by 10-15%/year
- Review and rebalance portfolio annually
- Life insurance review: Increase cover with salary increase
- Retirement planning: Should have ~2-3x annual income saved by 40

AGE 46-60 (Pre-Retirement):
- Shift equity to debt gradually (subtract age from 100 = equity %)
- Age 50 → 50% equity, 50% debt
- NPS: Can choose equity max 50% after 50 years
- Health insurance: Increase cover; costs rise with age
- Debt-free goal: Clear all loans before retirement

RETIREMENT (60+):
- Safe withdrawal rate: 4% of corpus per year (25x rule)
- SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan): Monthly withdrawal from MF
- SCSS: Up to ₹30L in Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (8.2%)
- Annuity from NPS: Guaranteed income but low returns (5-6%)
- Health: Ensure adequate cover; medical costs are primary retirement expense
