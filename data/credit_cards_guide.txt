CREDIT CARDS AND CIBIL SCORE GUIDE FOR INDIA

CREDIT CARD BASICS:
A credit card gives you a credit limit (e.g., ₹1 lakh) that you can spend and repay later.
Billing cycle: Usually 30 days. Statement date + 15-20 days = due date.
Interest-free period: Up to 50-55 days if you pay the full bill by due date.
Interest if you carry a balance: 24-48% per annum — one of the most expensive forms of debt in India.

GOLDEN RULES OF CREDIT CARDS:
1. ALWAYS pay the full statement balance by due date. Never just the minimum amount.
2. Minimum payment is a trap: ₹30,000 balance at 3% minimum payment at 36% interest takes 8+ years to clear.
3. Keep credit utilization below 30% of your limit. Example: Limit ₹1 lakh → use less than ₹30,000.
4. Don't use credit card for cash advance — charges 2.5-3.5% transaction fee + 42% p.a. interest from day 1.
5. Set up auto-pay for full amount to never miss due date.

REWARDS AND CASHBACK:
Cashback cards: Return 1-5% of spending as cashback. Good for general spending.
Travel cards: Reward points redeemable for flights, hotel stays. Good for frequent travelers.
Fuel surcharge waiver: Cards with fuel waiver save 1% surcharge on fuel.

BEST CREDIT CARDS IN INDIA (category-wise):
- For beginners/students: IDFC First Select, Axis Ace (cashback on UPI/bills)
- For cashback: Amazon Pay ICICI (5% on Amazon, 1% elsewhere)
- For travel: HDFC Infinia, SBI Vistara (Air India miles)
- For shopping: Flipkart Axis, Amazon Pay ICICI
- For fuel: BPCL SBI, Indian Oil Axis

CREDIT CARD EMI:
Banks offer to convert large purchases to EMI (No Cost EMI or Regular EMI).
No Cost EMI: Interest is hidden in processing fee or discount removal — not truly free.
Regular EMI: Interest of 12-16% per year.
Advice: Use EMI only if absolutely necessary. Otherwise pay full amount.

CIBIL SCORE — WHAT IT IS:
CIBIL score is a 3-digit credit score ranging from 300 to 900.
Generated by TransUnion CIBIL based on your credit history.
Used by banks to decide whether to approve loans and at what interest rate.
Score interpretation:
- 750-900: Excellent — best loan rates, easy approval
- 700-749: Good — approved with decent rates
- 650-699: Fair — may face higher interest rates
- 600-649: Poor — likely rejection or very high rates
- Below 600: Very poor — most loans will be rejected

HOW CIBIL SCORE IS CALCULATED:
- Payment history (35%): Do you pay EMIs and credit card bills on time?
- Credit utilization (30%): What percentage of credit limit do you use?
- Credit age (15%): How old are your credit accounts?
- Credit mix (10%): Do you have both secured (home loan) and unsecured (credit card) credit?
- New credit inquiries (10%): How often do you apply for new credit?

HOW TO IMPROVE CIBIL SCORE:
1. Pay all EMIs and credit card bills on time — this is the single most important factor
2. Keep credit card utilization below 30% (below 10% is ideal)
3. Don't close your oldest credit card — it reduces credit age
4. Avoid applying for multiple loans/cards in a short period (each inquiry reduces score)
5. If you have a bad loan, settle or pay it off — even settled accounts are better than defaults
6. Check your CIBIL report for errors at cibil.com — dispute errors if found

RECOVERING FROM BAD CIBIL SCORE:
- Takes 6 months to 2 years of consistent good behavior
- Get a secured credit card (against FD) to rebuild credit
- Take a small personal loan and repay on time
- Never default on any current obligations

CREDIT SCORE FREE CHECKS:
- CIBIL: cibil.com (1 free check per year)
- Paisabazaar, BankBazaar, CreditMantri: Free checks
- Experian, Equifax, CRIF also provide reports

WARNING SIGNS OF DEBT TRAP:
- Using one credit card to pay another
- Using credit card to pay for groceries and daily expenses consistently
- Only paying minimum amount due every month
- EMI taking more than 50% of your income
- Taking personal loan to pay off credit card debt repeatedly
