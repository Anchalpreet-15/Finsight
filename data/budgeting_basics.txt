BUDGETING BASICS FOR INDIANS — Apna Paisa Samajhna

Budgeting simply means planning where your money goes before it goes there. Most Indians wonder why their salary vanishes within 10 days of payday — a budget fixes exactly that.

UNDERSTANDING YOUR INDIAN SALARY (CTC vs In-Hand)
Your CTC (Cost to Company) is NOT what you actually receive. Your in-hand or take-home salary is lower because of:
- EPF deduction: 12% of your basic salary goes to Employee Provident Fund (employer also contributes 12%)
- Professional Tax: deducted by state governments (varies, usually ₹200/month)
- TDS: Tax Deducted at Source based on your income slab
- HRA (House Rent Allowance): shown in CTC but partially tax-exempt if you pay rent

Always budget based on your actual in-hand salary, not your CTC.

THE 50/30/20 RULE — INDIAN EDITION
A simple starting framework:
- 50% for Needs: rent, groceries, EMIs, utility bills, transport, school fees
- 30% for Wants: dining out, shopping, OTT subscriptions, outings, gadgets
- 20% for Savings and Investments: SIP, PPF, RD, emergency fund, FD

Example — in-hand salary of ₹40,000:
- ₹20,000 for needs
- ₹12,000 for wants
- ₹8,000 for savings and investments

ZERO-BASED BUDGETING
Assign every rupee a purpose. Take your monthly income and allocate it all — ₹0 left unassigned. This doesn't mean spending it all; it means every rupee is deliberately directed somewhere: groceries, SIP, emergency fund, fun money.

COMMON INDIAN BUDGET DRAINS
- Zomato/Swiggy orders silently adding ₹3,000–5,000/month
- OTT subscriptions (Netflix, Prime, Hotstar, Spotify) — ₹1,500–2,000/month combined if unmanaged
- Impulse shopping during Big Billion Days, End of Reason Sale, Great Indian Festival
- Festival expenses (Diwali gifts, new clothes, Navratri) — plan these months in advance
- Wedding season: gifts, travel, new outfits — budget ₹10,000–₹30,000 annually for shaadi season
- Sending money home (for migrants) — essential, but plan it as a fixed line item

TRACKING TOOLS POPULAR IN INDIA
- Walnut app — auto-tracks from bank SMS and categorises spends
- ET Money — tracks SIPs, expenses, and tax-saving investments
- Money Manager — simple manual tracking
- Google Sheets — fully customisable and free

PRACTICAL FIRST STEPS
1. Note your exact in-hand salary this month
2. List all fixed monthly outflows: rent, EMIs, SIP, subscriptions
3. Estimate variable expenses: groceries, petrol, eating out
4. Set a savings target — even ₹500/month invested in a SIP builds a habit
5. Review at month-end — where did the money actually go?

Budgeting is not about restriction — it is about making sure your hard-earned rupees go where you actually want them to go.
