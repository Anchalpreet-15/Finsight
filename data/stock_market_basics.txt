STOCK MARKET BASICS FOR INDIAN INVESTORS

WHAT IS THE STOCK MARKET:
A stock market is a marketplace where shares (ownership stakes) of companies are bought and sold.
India has two major exchanges: NSE (National Stock Exchange) and BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange).
Nifty 50: Top 50 companies on NSE by market capitalization. Benchmark index.
Sensex: Top 30 companies on BSE. Oldest index in India.

HOW TO START INVESTING IN STOCKS:
Step 1: Open a Demat account (stores shares electronically) + Trading account
Brokers: Zerodha (cheapest for frequent traders, ₹20/trade), Groww (free delivery trades), Angel One, HDFC Securities, ICICI Direct
Step 2: Link to your bank account
Step 3: Add funds and start buying
Step 4: Buy shares through the broker's app/platform

DEMAT ACCOUNT CHARGES:
Account opening: Free on most platforms (Zerodha, Groww)
Annual maintenance charge (AMC): ₹300-900/year depending on broker
Brokerage: Zero for delivery trades on Groww; ₹20 flat per trade on Zerodha

STOCK INVESTMENT APPROACHES:

Buy and Hold (Long-term investing):
- Buy fundamentally strong companies and hold for 5-10+ years
- Warren Buffett's approach
- Works well for large, established companies (Reliance, TCS, HDFC Bank, Infosys)
- Requires patience but historically rewarding

Value Investing:
- Buy stocks trading below their intrinsic value
- Requires fundamental analysis (reading balance sheets, P/E ratio, debt levels)
- Not recommended for beginners

Growth Investing:
- Invest in companies growing revenue and profits rapidly
- Higher risk, higher potential reward
- Example: Small/mid-cap companies in growing sectors

SIP in Stocks (through mutual funds or directly):
- More beginner-friendly through index funds that track Nifty 50
- Less risky than picking individual stocks

KEY RATIOS TO UNDERSTAND:
P/E Ratio (Price to Earnings): Stock price / Earnings per share. Lower can mean cheaper, but context matters.
Market Cap: Total market value of company. Large cap = stable, Small cap = risky.
Dividend: Share of profit paid to shareholders. Dividend yield = Dividend / Share price.

WHAT IS F&O (FUTURES & OPTIONS):
Derivatives that derive value from an underlying stock or index.
EXTREMELY RISKY: Over 90% of F&O traders lose money.
Never trade F&O without full understanding. It's essentially speculation, not investing.
SEBI study (2023): 89% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY 2021-22.

INDEX FUNDS vs DIRECT STOCKS:
Index funds are BETTER for most investors because:
- Automatic diversification across 50+ companies
- No need to research individual companies
- Lower cost (expense ratio 0.05-0.2%)
- Has beaten most actively managed funds over 10+ years
- No emotional decision-making required

For beginners: Start with Nifty 50 index fund SIP before picking individual stocks.

TAX ON STOCK GAINS:
Short-term capital gains (held < 1 year): 15% flat
Long-term capital gains (held > 1 year): 10% on gains above ₹1,00,000 per year (tax-free up to ₹1L)
Dividend income: Added to your income and taxed at your slab rate

COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID:
1. Panic selling during market crashes — markets always recover over time
2. Investing money you need in 1-2 years in equity
3. Trading F&O without experience — most people lose money
4. Following tips on social media / WhatsApp without research
5. Checking portfolio daily — causes anxiety and bad decisions
6. Putting all money in one stock — diversify
7. Timing the market — even experts can't do this consistently

CRYPTOCURRENCY IN INDIA:
30% flat tax on all crypto gains (no deductions allowed, no setoff against losses)
1% TDS on crypto transactions above ₹10,000
High risk, highly speculative
SEBI and RBI have expressed concerns — not regulated like stocks
Limit to maximum 5-10% of portfolio if you invest at all
Never borrow to invest in crypto
